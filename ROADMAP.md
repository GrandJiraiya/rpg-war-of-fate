# Roadmap

The playable slice is Act I, Day 1 on the Reed Causeway. It keeps the prototype's combat, oaths, and geography. The book site (waroffate.com) describes a wider war. These are the gaps to close later, taken from that story bible. None of them are in this build on purpose.

## Numbers and oath pressure

The book site treats echo as pressure, and says nothing on the page has a level. This game uses echoes as a currency, Echo as a stat, and a level bought at a hearth. That is the prototype's call. Changing it means reworking combat, the death pile, and the hearth, not a content pass.

## The Nine Glasses

Only six oaths are playable: Marsh, Shard, Dawn, Frost, Choir, and Wind. The Glasses that still need a verb, a cost, and a god are:

- DawnGlass / Holy — Arcolos. The prototype's Dawn Oath is Pale Dawn steel, not House of Cock's holy oath.
- VoidGlass — Yezmir. Nezz hears him. Void is not a sworn oath.
- AshGlass / Fire — Vaethra. Not represented.
- FrostGlass — Frostmere. The Frost Oath is close, and it is sworn to House of Hen.
- WindGlass — Vendent Thorn. The Wind Oath is close, and it is sworn to Greenwood.
- LiquidGlass / Water — H'to-O. The Marsh Oath is the nearest thing, sworn to Wet Peck.
- StoneGlass / Earth — the Wells. They appear as priests, casters, and the Stone Cantor, not as a player oath.
- PowerGlass / Lightning — Legless's second oath. Not a player oath.
- VibeGlass / Sound — the Choir Oath is the nearest thing. Zilla's sound oath is absent.

Breaking an oath has no consequence yet.

## Houses that do not match their Glass

- House of Cock is Holy / DawnGlass in the bible. Here the Cock soldier swears the Shard Oath.
- The Choir fights for the causeway. Benedict, Voice of the Choir, is a Pale Dawn general.
- House Greenwood's oath is to take no part in wars. The Wind Oath still fields a causeway soldier.
- Pale Dawn's Legless is Wind, then Lightning, and carries Dawn's Edge. The Pale player oath is holy-flavored steel, and the blade in the pack is explicitly not the real Dawn's Edge.

## People who need a canon check

The causeway uses three names the bible does not: **Bix** (First Guard; the day-one messenger in the book is Karl's son **Drix**), **Steward Tal** (the book's steward is **Halvar**), and **Sila** at DeepWell. Karl, Nezz, Goss, and Orin match.

## Cards

Eighteen portraits open from the title screen. The bible's deck has twenty-nine. Missing from the gallery: Oridan, Queen Lex, Orin, and the Glass gods other than Arcolos and Yezmir (Vaethra, Frostmere, Vendent Thorn, H'to-O, Halde, Vibeon, Caelian), plus Ovarion.

Card roles and the four abilities on each card are not mechanics. There is no formation, no squad, and no siege action for Command Shout, Holy Shield, or Marsh Command.

## Choice

Dialogue is accept or leave. The bible's choice-trace, Restraint Recognized, Cost of Survival, Truth is Expensive, and the Pi Gates that judge a person are not in the slice. The Pi Gate under Cock Fortress is not a place you can reach.

## The rest of the war

One day of forty, and one act of eight. Cock Fortress and the Deepwell pit sit a short walk from the marsh. Pilgrim's Gate, Zilla, the Echo Dome, Kockenflocker, and the other seats are pins on Talon's map and nothing else.

The soldiers are procedural rigs of boxes, cones, and cylinders. The book art is painterly roosters, hens, and beasts. Replacing the rigs is an art pass, not a logic pass.

There is no scored music. The book site has one track per act. This build uses short synthesized tones only.
